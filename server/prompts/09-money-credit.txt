# category: Money, credit and debt
# blurb: Budgets, debt payoff, credit building, saving and money habits. AI can organize and explain; it isn't a financial advisor, so check big decisions with a professional.

## Build my zero-based budget
You are a budgeting coach (not a financial advisor). My take-home pay is [MONTHLY INCOME, or list each paycheck and date]. My bills and spending are: [LIST WITH AMOUNTS].
Build a zero-based budget where every dollar has a job: needs, debts, savings, and fun. Show it as a table.
Point out where I'm overspending compared to common guidelines, suggest 5 realistic cuts, and give me a weekly 10-minute check-in routine. Ask me about irregular expenses (car repairs, gifts, annual fees) and add a sinking fund for each.

## Debt payoff plan: avalanche vs snowball
Act as a debt payoff coach. My debts are: [LIST: name, balance, interest rate, minimum payment]. I can put [EXTRA AMOUNT] extra toward debt each month.
Calculate the payoff order, timeline and total interest for both the avalanche method (highest rate first) and the snowball method (smallest balance first). Show month-by-month for the first 6 months.
Recommend one based on my personality (ask me 2 questions first), and tell me what to do with each freed-up payment.

## Understand my credit report
You are a credit education coach. I'll paste the sections of my credit report (with account numbers removed): [PASTE].
Explain in plain English what each part means, what's helping my score (on-time history, low utilization, account age), what's hurting it, and which items look like they might be errors.
For any item that looks wrong, explain my right to dispute inaccurate information with the credit bureau and the company that reported it. Don't suggest disputing information that is accurate.

## Write a credit report dispute letter for an error
Act as a consumer rights writing assistant (not a lawyer). This item on my credit report is wrong: [DESCRIBE THE ITEM AND WHY IT'S INACCURATE, e.g. not my account, paid but shows unpaid, wrong late date].
Write a clear dispute letter to [BUREAU NAME] that identifies me (I'll fill in my details), the item, why it's inaccurate, what I want corrected, and a list of the proof I'm attaching.
Tell me how to send it (online portal or certified mail), what timeline to expect, and how to follow up. Only dispute things that are actually inaccurate.

## Goodwill letter for a late payment
You are a letter-writing assistant. I had a late payment with [LENDER] on my [ACCOUNT TYPE] in [MONTH YEAR] because [REASON, e.g. a hospital stay, a job change, an autopay mix-up], and I've paid on time every month since [DATE].
Write a short, sincere goodwill letter asking them to consider removing the late mark as a courtesy. Include: a thank-you for being a customer, a brief honest explanation, what I've done to prevent it happening again, and a polite request.
Explain that this is a request, not a right, that they may say no, and how to send it (the lender's customer service address or secure message). Give me a shorter version I can use in a phone call.

## Raise my credit score plan
Act as a credit-building coach. My situation: score around [SCORE IF KNOWN], cards and limits [LIST], balances [LIST], late payments [ANY], collections [ANY], and how long I've had credit.
Make a 90-day plan using legitimate steps: paying on time (autopay minimums), lowering credit utilization (show the target balances per card), not closing old cards, checking reports for errors, and becoming an authorized user or using a secured card or credit-builder loan if it fits.
Explain what usually moves a score and how long changes take. Warn me about "credit repair" offers that promise to remove accurate negative items or sell new credit identities, because those are scams.

## Credit utilization calculator
