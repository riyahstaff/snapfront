# category: Digital products that sell
# blurb: Find, outline, write and package digital products: guides, planners, templates, printables, courses and more. Most end by drafting the product in Snapfront.

## 15 digital product ideas from one skill
You are a digital product strategist. My skill is [YOUR SKILL], my experience level is [BEGINNER / INTERMEDIATE / EXPERT], and the people I could help are [AUDIENCE].
Give me 15 digital product ideas. For each one list: who buys it, the problem it solves, the format (PDF guide, template, checklist, workbook, mini course, spreadsheet), how hard it is to make (1 to 5), how fast I could launch it, and a fair price range.
Rank them by "easiest to make and most likely to sell". Then ask me which one to build, and when I pick, outline it and draft it in my Snapfront store with create_draft_product.

## Mine my own knowledge for products
Before suggesting anything, interview me. Ask me 10 questions, one at a time, about: things friends ask me for help with, problems I've solved for myself, tools and routines I use daily, mistakes I made and fixed, and topics I could talk about for an hour.
After the interview, give me 10 digital product ideas based only on my answers, each with a title, who it's for, the format and a price. Mark the 3 strongest and say why.

## The product ladder
You are a product ecosystem strategist. My skill or niche is [NICHE], my audience size is [ROUGH AUDIENCE SIZE], and the main transformation I help with is [RESULT].
Design a 4-step product ladder:
1. A low-priced starter ($5 to $15)
2. A core product ($27 to $97)
3. A premium offer ($150+, like a course, a done-for-you pack or a coaching add-on)
4. A recurring option (a membership, monthly templates or a club)
For each step give the title, format, promise, price and what naturally leads the buyer to the next step. Then draft the starter product in Snapfront with create_draft_product.

## Validate demand before I build
Act as a market validation researcher. My product idea is [IDEA] for [AUDIENCE].
Score it from 1 to 10 on: how painful the problem is, how often people search for it, whether people already pay for solutions, how easy it is to reach these buyers, and how quickly I can make it.
Then give me 3 ways to test demand in 7 days without building the full product (for example a waitlist post, a pre-sale, a poll or a free sample) with the exact text for each test. Tell me what result would mean "build it" and what would mean "drop it".
